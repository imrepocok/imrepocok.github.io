Le Wagon Bootcamp · 2025

Neo Bank —
User Engagement & Retention Analysis

During the Le Wagon Data Analytics Bootcamp, we received a dataset from a European neo-bank — a digital-first bank known for fee-free cross-currency transactions. Our task was to analyse user behaviour, understand what drives engagement, and identify where churn risk is highest.

The full dataset — covering ~19,000 users, 2.7M transactions, and 16 months of activity — was loaded into Google BigQuery. We built four analytical views in SQL, then designed a six-page interactive report in Looker Studio to tell the story of how users activate, engage, and retain.

03
Retention
Retention is Neo Bank's strongest signal. Across every cohort, roughly 70–80% of users are active in their first month — but those who stay settle into a stable plateau around 55–60%, holding steady for over a year. The real churn risk isn't long-term attrition: it's the small population of users who never activate at all, concentrated in specific markets.

06
Key findings
Seven findings emerged from this analysis — each grounded in the data and directly actionable.
Finding 01
Churn is an activation problem, not a retention one
Users who complete at least one transaction are remarkably sticky — retention plateaus at 55–60% and holds flat for over a year. The real risk is the 4.6% who never activate at all, not long-term attrition among engaged users.
Evidence
Month-12 retention (55.3%) is almost identical to month-6 (55.4%) across all cohorts. Ghost users average 2.5 declined card attempts — they tried, but something blocked them.
Churn Risk Product
Finding 02
Crypto unlocking is the strongest engagement predictor
Users who unlock the crypto feature in-app transact 72% more than non-crypto Standard users, and the pattern holds across every plan tier. It's likely a proxy for "power user mindset" — someone who explores the app deeply also uses it more.
Evidence
Crypto-unlocked Standard users: avg 159.8 transactions. Non-crypto Standard users: avg 92.8 transactions. Only 18% of users have unlocked crypto.
Product Growth
Finding 03
Early card declines kill engagement
Low-engagement users have a 30% card decline rate — nearly 1 in 3 attempts fails. Power users decline at just 7%. Reducing early friction (insufficient funds, card not activated, unclear UX) is likely the single highest-leverage lever for converting low-engagement users into medium or power users.
Evidence
Ghost users: 100% decline rate (tried, never succeeded). Low: 29.9%. Medium: 13.1%. Power: 7.0%.
Product Churn Risk
Finding 04
Greece and Lithuania are structurally weak markets
Greece has the lowest activation rate in the dataset (78.9%), the highest ghost rate (21.1%), and the slowest time-to-first-transaction (40 days). Lithuania mirrors this pattern. Both suggest onboarding friction or trust barriers specific to these markets, not a lack of demand.
Evidence
Greece activation: 78.9% vs dataset avg 95.7%. Ghost rate: 21.1% vs UK 3.0%. Avg days to first tx: 40.1 vs dataset avg 15.7.
Market Churn Risk
Finding 05
Malta and Ireland are model markets
Both markets combine near-perfect activation (98%+), low ghost rates, fast time-to-first-transaction, and above-average premium plan uptake. Understanding what makes onboarding work in these markets — and replicating it elsewhere — is the clearest geographic growth opportunity.
Evidence
Malta: 98.2% activation, 0.9% ghost rate, 20.3 days avg. Ireland: 97.9% activation, 1.6% ghost rate, 16.5 days avg.
Market Growth
Finding 06
FX is core, not peripheral
Cross-currency exchange accounts for $30M of the $149M total transaction volume — 20% of all money moved on the platform. The UK drives FX by volume, but Greek users make far larger individual FX transactions ($772 avg vs $270 for UK), suggesting a niche high-value use case worth investigating.
Evidence
EXCHANGE: $30.4M completed volume. Greece avg FX tx: $772.5. Next highest: Netherlands at $343.6.
Product Market
Finding 07
Notification opt-in does not predict engagement
Users who opted into both push and email marketing notifications have a ghost rate of 6.5% — higher than fully opted-out users at 1.3%. Engagement drives opt-in, not the reverse. This has implications for how re-engagement campaigns are designed: the opted-in audience is not the engaged audience.
Evidence
Push + email opted-in: ghost rate 6.5%, avg 132 tx. Both opted-out: ghost rate 1.3%, avg 112 tx.
